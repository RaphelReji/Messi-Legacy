FROM ROSARIO TO THE WORLD
Lionel Andrés Messi was born on June 24, 1987, in Rosario, Argentina, the third of four children in a football-mad family. He started playing almost as soon as he could walk, turning out for local club Grandoli before joining Newell's Old Boys' youth academy at age six. At eleven, Messi was diagnosed with a growth hormone deficiency. The treatment was expensive, and when his family's circumstances made it hard to keep paying for it, FC Barcelona stepped in — famously agreeing terms on a napkin — and brought a 13-year-old Messi and his family to Spain in 2000. He joined La Masia, Barcelona's academy, and never looked back. He made his first-team debut for Barcelona in 2004 at 17, and by his early twenties was already being spoken of alongside the greatest players in the sport's history. Across 17 seasons at Barcelona, six European Golden Shoes, ten La Liga titles, and four Champions League trophies followed, along with a Guinness World Record for most goals scored in a calendar year (91, in 2012). After leaving Barcelona in 2021, Messi spent two seasons at Paris Saint-Germain, where — in between club football — he finally won the one trophy that had eluded him: the FIFA World Cup, lifted with Argentina in Qatar in December 2022, arguably completing the greatest individual résumé the sport has ever seen. In 2023, Messi surprised much of the football world by joining Inter Miami CF in Major League Soccer, kicking off what's been called the "Messi effect" on American soccer — sold-out stadiums, record TV audiences, and a trophy (the Leagues Cup) within his first month in the league.

BEYOND THE PITCH
Messi has run the Leo Messi Foundation since 2007, supporting healthcare and education access for children — a cause shaped by his own childhood experience with medical treatment. He's also one of the most commercially recognizable athletes on the planet, with a long-running partnership with Adidas and ventures spanning hospitality, media, and youth football academies bearing his name.
